# Career Transition Analysis

**[中文](README.md) | [English](README_EN.md)**

> Stop asking “What career am I suited for?” in the abstract. Use evidence from your experience to answer: **Where can I move, why, what am I missing, and what should I do next?**

[![License: MIT](https://img.shields.io/badge/License-MIT-blue.svg)](LICENSE)
[![Codex Skill](https://img.shields.io/badge/Codex-Skill-black)](SKILL.md)

Career Transition Analysis is an open-source Agent Skill for career changers and career practitioners. It turns personal manuals, résumés, project histories, target roles, and real job requirements into a traceable and actionable career-transition report.

It is not a personality test, and it does not stop at generic observations such as “you have strong communication skills.” It works more like a research-oriented career strategist: establish the facts, extract transferable capabilities, compare them with market requirements, identify the critical gaps, and turn the result into a practical transition path.

## What you will get

A complete run can produce nine deliverables:

1. **Personal fact ledger** — education, roles, projects, outcomes, tools, domain exposure, and constraints, separated into facts, inferences, and open questions.
2. **Capability asset map** — transferable capabilities extracted from evidence rather than inferred from job titles.
3. **Target-role portfolio** — usually 3–5 comparable options instead of one premature recommendation.
4. **Role-match matrix** — why each role fits, what evidence supports it, and where the risks are.
5. **Prioritized gap analysis** — hard skills, domain knowledge, business context, proof of work, and positioning gaps.
6. **Direct and bridge paths** — roles you can pursue now and stepping-stone roles or projects that reduce a larger transition risk.
7. **Job-search asset plan** — portfolio pieces, case studies, résumé bullets, and interview stories to build.
8. **30/60/90-day roadmap** — learning, proof-building, networking, applications, and feedback in one plan.
9. **Scenario-based prediction** — roles you can reasonably target now, after closing critical gaps, and after gaining adjacent experience. It does not invent a precise probability of being hired.

```text
Career history
      ↓
Fact check → Capability extraction → Role matching → Gap prioritization
      ↑                                      ↓
      └──── Market feedback ← Asset building ← Transition plan
```

## Who this is for

### A strong fit

- Professionals with roughly 1–10 years of experience who want to change function, industry, or enter an emerging field.
- People with substantial experience who struggle to translate it into the language of a target role.
- People comparing several directions and wanting to understand trade-offs, opportunity cost, and accessibility.
- Professionals moving from operations, marketing, consulting, research, or project management into AI, product, strategy, growth, or solutions roles.
- Career coaches, recruiters, and advisors who want a repeatable, evidence-based analysis process.
- People who want to identify what they truly need to learn before paying for courses or credentials.

### Not a direct fit

- Transitions into licensed or legally regulated professions without first checking the relevant local requirements.
- Anyone looking for a single “destined career” based only on personality or interests.
- Anyone unwilling to build evidence through projects, work samples, or practical experience.
- Anyone expecting a guaranteed offer or a fabricated success percentage.

## The four questions it solves

| Stage | The user's question | What the Skill provides |
|---|---|---|
| Extraction | What am I actually capable of? | Capabilities, assets, preferences, and constraints grounded in experience |
| Matching | Which roles can I transition into? | Comparative role analysis plus direct and bridge paths |
| Gap | What do I need to close? | Gaps ranked by impact, urgency, and cost to close |
| Prediction | How far can this path take me? | Evidence-based scenarios for near-term and later-stage roles |

## Step by step

### Step 0: Build the fact ledger

**You provide:** a personal manual, résumé, project history, work samples, target interests, and practical constraints.

**The Skill does:** separates confirmed facts from reasonable inferences and missing information. If inputs conflict, it asks before treating an assumption as fact.

**You receive:** a traceable evidence base that supports every later recommendation.

### Step 1: Extraction — identify underlying capabilities

The analysis looks beyond titles and examines four layers:

- **Tasks:** what you repeatedly did.
- **Capabilities:** how you did it—research, analysis, coordination, selling, decision-making, or delivery.
- **Evidence:** scale, complexity, outcomes, and external recognition.
- **Boundaries:** interests, risk tolerance, location, compensation, time, and non-negotiables.

The output distinguishes demonstrated strengths from capabilities that may exist but still lack proof.

### Step 2: Matching — compare roles and paths

You can name a target field or ask the Skill to generate options. In a full analysis, it should compare your evidence with real job descriptions or current public hiring information, including:

- similarity of core work;
- evidence for must-have capabilities;
- time required to cross domain barriers;
- whether employers can understand your current profile;
- whether a bridge role is safer than a direct move.

Three gates shape the decision:

1. **Fact Gate:** Is there enough personal evidence to make the judgment?
2. **Market Gate:** Does the target role exist in a meaningful and stable form?
3. **Accessibility Gate:** Is direct entry realistic with the evidence available today?

### Step 3: Gap — prioritize what truly matters

A useful gap analysis is not a list of courses. Each gap must answer:

1. Will it block interviews or on-the-job delivery?
2. Is the capability missing, or is only the evidence missing?
3. What is the fastest credible way to demonstrate it?
4. Will closing it materially improve access to the target role?

A practical order is: **entry requirements → core delivery capability → proof of work → domain language → optional advantages**.

### Step 4: Build — turn gaps into job-search assets

The Skill converts recommendations into visible evidence, such as:

- a target-industry or competitor analysis;
- an end-to-end AI or product case based on a real business problem;
- a portfolio showing process, decisions, and results;
- revised résumé bullets;
- STAR or CARE interview stories;
- a small, measurable application experiment.

### Step 5: Prediction — make conditional role forecasts

Predictions are expressed as scenarios, not fake precision:

- **Current state:** the roles you can credibly pursue now and the main risks.
- **After critical gaps are closed:** how your reachable role set changes after specific proof or skills are added.
- **After adjacent experience:** which higher-level or more direct roles become plausible after one relevant experience cycle.

Each conclusion should include the evidence, confidence level, assumptions, and new information that could overturn it.

## A short example

**Starting profile:** Four years in B2B customer operations, including discovery interviews, solution coordination, renewals, and cross-functional delivery. The person wants to move into AI product management but has never held a product-manager title.

**Generic advice:** Take a product course, learn AI, and apply to more jobs.

**This Skill goes further:**

- Existing assets: customer discovery, complex stakeholder communication, project execution, and commercial awareness.
- Missing evidence: product definition, metrics, prototyping, and technical trade-off decisions.
- Direct paths: AI customer success, AI solutions, or product operations.
- Bridge path: enter a customer-facing role close to product, then use internal projects to move toward AI product management.
- Critical work sample: choose a real business problem and produce user research, problem framing, workflow, metrics, and an MVP prototype.
- 90-day validation: build the case → interview five practitioners → run targeted applications across two role families → update the gaps using interview feedback.

The value is not merely naming a role. It is turning an apparently unrelated past into an evidence chain the target market understands.

## Three ways to use it

### 1. Quick diagnosis

```text
Use career-transition-analysis to assess the background below.
First build a fact ledger and identify missing information. Then extract my
underlying capabilities and propose 3–5 target roles. Before creating a long
action plan, compare fit, major risks, and direct versus bridge paths.

[Paste your background]
```

### 2. Run the full process

```text
Use career-transition-analysis to run the complete process on my personal manual:
Fact Check → Extraction → Matching → Gap → Asset Building → Prediction.
Separate facts, inferences, and recommendations. Compare my profile with real
role requirements. Produce a complete transition report with a role-match matrix,
prioritized gaps, a job-search asset plan, and a 30/60/90-day roadmap.

Target industry or role: [fill in, or write "help me explore"]
Constraints: [location, compensation, time, risk tolerance, etc.]
[Paste or attach your materials]
```

### 3. Compare your profile with real job descriptions

```text
Compare my background with the three job descriptions below.
Do not rely on keyword matching. Separate requirements into:
demonstrated with evidence, likely but unproven, clearly missing, and unnecessary.
Then rank the roles and recommend résumé changes, interview-risk preparation,
and the minimum gap-closing plan.

[Background]
[Job description 1]
[Job description 2]
[Job description 3]
```

## Prepare your personal manual

Your input does not need to be polished. It needs to be concrete. At minimum, include:

- education, roles, years of experience, and industries;
- 3–5 projects with your actions and measurable results;
- hard skills, tools, transferable capabilities, and work samples;
- target interests and reasons for changing;
- location, compensation, time, risk, and other non-negotiable constraints.

Copy the [complete English personal manual template](examples/personal-manual-template-en.md) to get started.

## How it differs from a typical career test

| Typical approach | Career Transition Analysis |
|---|---|
| Infers careers from personality labels | Extracts capabilities from real work and outcomes |
| Focuses on what you like | Combines motivation, evidence, market demand, and constraints |
| Produces a long list of jobs | Compares 3–5 directions and explains the trade-offs |
| Treats every weakness as a skill gap | Separates capability, evidence, and positioning gaps |
| Ends with course recommendations | Converts learning into work samples and market tests |
| Presents confident answers | States assumptions, confidence, and disconfirming evidence |

## Operating principles

- **Evidence first:** keep facts, inferences, and recommendations distinct.
- **Market validation:** use real job descriptions and current public hiring evidence for a full analysis.
- **Role portfolio:** compare several directions instead of making a single early bet.
- **Executable paths:** recommendations should become projects, work samples, conversations, or applications.
- **Capability is not evidence:** being able to do something and being able to prove it are different problems.
- **Privacy:** remove names, contact details, employer-confidential information, and unrelated sensitive data before sharing public examples or Issues.

## Limits

- This is a decision-support tool. It does not replace employer judgment or guarantee an offer.
- Compensation, demand, and role definitions change; time-sensitive conclusions should be researched again.
- Better evidence produces better analysis. If the input is incomplete, the Skill should ask questions first.
- Prediction means conditional, evidence-based scenarios—not a statistical hiring probability.
